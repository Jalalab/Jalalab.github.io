ChainGuardFL: can you trust the data inside a federated model?

A federated learning framework that screens every client's images for AI-generated fakes and duplicates once, before training starts, and logs every decision to a tamper-evident ledger.

The problem

Federated learning trusts every client blindly.

In federated learning, clients train locally and only share model updates. That protects privacy, but the server never sees what each client actually trained on. Standard FedAvg assumes every image is genuine.

Image generators make that assumption weak. We looked at two specific threats in an art style classification setting:

Fake contamination. A client's dataset contains AI-generated artwork, by accident or on purpose.
Cross-client duplication. The same painting, re-saved at a different size or format, enters through several clients and quietly gets extra weight.

On top of both, nobody except the server operator can verify afterwards what was checked or rejected.

The approach

Check once, before round 1.

ChainGuardFL is not a new aggregation rule. It is a fixed preprocessing stage in front of ordinary FedAvg, so its cost is paid once instead of every round.

DaViT fake filter

A DaViT-Base real/fake discriminator scores every training image. Anything above τ = 0.5 is dropped.

Pixel-hash registry

Images are converted to RGB, resized to 256×256 (Lanczos) and hashed with SHA-256. A matching hash from another client is rejected, whatever the file format.

Federated training

10 non-IID clients (Dirichlet α = 0.5) train on their fixed, verified shards for 50 rounds with FedAvg.

SHA-256 audit ledger

Every filter verdict, registry decision, weight-update hash and global model hash is chained into a ledger that anyone can re-verify.

The filter

Useful, but not perfect, and the report says so.

0recall: 1,965 of 2,407 fake training images caught
0precision: 843 genuine images were wrongly removed
0of the 7,575 training images were removed overall
Honest reading. The filter recovers about a third of the damage (2.15 of 6.77 points). The remaining gap to centralized training averages 9.73 points: roughly 5.11 from federated non-IID training itself, and 4.62 from contamination the filter misses. The 31.7% round-time saving exists because 37.1% of images are removed, so part of it is simply less data.

Limitations

Where it breaks.

One-shot filtering. The filter runs once, so any weakness against a new image generator affects the whole run.

Simulated ledger. The blockchain is a SHA-256 hash chain inside one process. It gives tamper evidence, not multi-node consensus.

Pixel-level duplicates only. Two different photos of the same painting are not caught.

Content, not updates. A client with clean data but a poisoned weight update would pass every check here.

One heterogeneity setting. Everything uses α = 0.5, so we can't say how the benefit changes under more extreme skew.
